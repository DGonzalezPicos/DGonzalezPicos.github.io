Data reduction & modelling

Reliable atmospheric inference depends as much on how spectra are extracted and calibrated as on the forward model. Much of my work sits at that interface: building reductions for faint companions, validating detections of weak isotopologues, and coupling flexible radiative-transfer models to Bayesian samplers.

Pipelines and extraction

  • For VLT/CRIRES+ programmes I use and contribute to reductions based on excalibuhr (dark/flat correction, order tracing, nodding, wavelength calibration).
  • For close-in companions (e.g. β Pic b), I developed a spectral-extraction scheme that co-adds spatial rows weighted by the planet’s cross-correlation strength after subtracting a jointly fitted stellar model — improving S/N when the target sits on a bright scattered-light background.
  • Archival SPIRou spectra for M dwarfs were quality-filtered (S/N, airmass, seeing) and coadded with barycentric alignment, with uncertainties that capture epoch-to-epoch variance.

Atmospheric retrievals

My standard modelling stack combines:

  1. Line-by-line radiative transfer with petitRADTRANS and up-to-date molecular/atomic opacities.
  2. Flexible P–T profiles (piecewise gradients at selected pressure nodes) rather than fixed grids alone.
  3. Chemistry from FastChem equilibrium, with optional logarithmic abundance offsets per species when the data demand it.
  4. Nested sampling to map posteriors and compare models with/without rare isotopologues via Bayesian evidence — the same framework used to validate $^{13}\mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{C}^{18}\mathrm{O}$ detections.
  5. For JWST companions, linear models of residual speckles or simultaneous disc continuum + CO slab emission when infrared excess contaminates the photosphere.

High-resolution spectroscopy

Resolving individual molecular lines transforms atmospheric characterisation: line depths and shapes constrain abundances and temperature structure, while line positions encode radial and rotational velocities. For isotope work, resolving $^{12}\mathrm{CO}$ from $^{13}\mathrm{CO}$ (and oxygen isotopologues) is often only possible at high resolving power.

Instruments and regimes

I work across complementary facilities:

  • VLT/CRIRES+ ($R\sim 10^{5}$) — Workhorse for the ESO SupJup Survey and for β Pic b. The K-band CO overtone region near 2.3 μm is particularly powerful for carbon isotopes.
  • JWST/NIRSpec ($R\sim 2700$) — Broad 1–5 μm coverage reaches the CO fundamental band near 4.6 μm, enabling simultaneous atmospheric and disc constraints that are difficult from the ground.
  • SPIRou / CFHT (archival) — High-S/N K-band spectra of nearby M dwarfs for Galactic chemical evolution studies.

My contribution

  • Built and applied Bayesian atmospheric retrievals coupling petitRADTRANS with nested sampling (e.g. MultiNest / UltraNest) to forward-model observed spectra.
  • Led multi-epoch CRIRES+ programmes where night-by-night retrievals are combined so that systematic epoch scatter enters the final error budget (β Pic b).
  • For close-in companions, developed extraction strategies that weight spatial rows by cross-correlation strength after modelling residual starlight — critical when the planet sits on a bright slit background.
  • Used JWST medium-resolution spectra to detect dozens of molecular and atomic species while simultaneously fitting warm disc continuum and optically thin CO emission (TWA 27A, TWA 28).

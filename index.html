Darío González Picos

PhD candidate in exoplanet atmospheres · Leiden Observatory

Hi, I’m Darío — I use high-resolution spectroscopy to measure the composition and isotope ratios of exoplanets, brown dwarfs, and cool stars, tracing how giant planets form and how the Galaxy enriches carbon and oxygen.

I work in the group of Prof. Ignas Snellen at Leiden Observatory (2023–2027). My PhD focuses on the ESO SupJup Survey, JWST/NIRSpec atmospheres, and M-dwarf compositional studies.

About me

My research focuses on high-resolution spectroscopy and atmospheric retrievals. I study the atmospheric composition of giant planets to understand their formation and evolution. I build tools for ground-based instruments like VLT/CRIRES+ to analyze observations of directly-imaged planets and brown dwarfs. I also use JWST/NIRSpec for atmospheric characterization. I am also interested in retrievals of M dwarf composition and their role as chemical tracers.

During my PhD I have contributed carbon-isotope measurements for imaged companions such as β Pic b and GQ Lup B, developed JWST high-contrast spectroscopy methods that account for residual starlight, and measured rare C and O isotopes across a sample of nearby M dwarfs.

Before Leiden I worked as a student support astronomer at the Isaac Newton Group of Telescopes on La Palma (2020–2021), and I continue to teach as a student teaching assistant at Leiden University.

Research highlights

Carbon isotopes in β Pic b

With eleven nights of VLT/CRIRES+ K-band spectroscopy we measure 12C/13C = 58+18−15 in the atmosphere of the young super-Jupiter β Pic b, together with near-solar metallicity and C/O. The ratio is consistent with the local ISM rather than extreme ice enrichment, placing a classic imaged planet on a firmer isotopic footing. (DOI · SciX)

Rare isotopes in nearby M dwarfs

Analysing SPIRou spectra of 32 nearby M dwarfs, we find that more metal-rich stars have lower 12C/13C, tracking progressive 13C enrichment of the interstellar medium. This Galactic baseline is essential for interpreting planetary isotope ratios as formation tracers. (DOI · SciX)

TRAPPIST-1 elemental and isotopic abundances

Joint Keck/NIRSPEC M-band and CFHT/SPIRou K-band spectroscopy yields the first stellar C/O and carbon- and oxygen-isotope ratios for the planet-host star TRAPPIST-1: solar-like metallicity and C/O, with 12C/13C = 154+17−16 and 16O/18O = 490+78−64 — a benchmark for future atmospheric studies of its planets. (DOI · SciX)

SupJup companions and atmospheric composition

In the ESO SupJup Survey we measure 12C/13C for young brown dwarfs and the GQ Lup system. GQ Lup B and its K7 host share indistinguishable carbon isotope ratios, favouring a shared natal reservoir over a chemically distinct pathway. (SupJup IV · SupJup II)

JWST atmospheres of brown dwarfs

Young brown dwarfs often show disc and atmospheric signatures mixed in the same JWST/NIRSpec spectrum; joint models are required to recover reliable isotope constraints. For the close-in T dwarf HD 19467 B, residual starlight can masquerade as atmospheric features unless they are modelled correctly. (DOI · HD 19467 B)

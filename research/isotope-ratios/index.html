Isotope ratios

Beyond bulk elemental abundances, isotopic ratios record nucleosynthesis, disc chemistry, and the balance of gas versus solid accretion. Carbon ($^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$) and oxygen ($^{16}\mathrm{O}/^{18}\mathrm{O}$) are especially accessible in cool atmospheres through CO and H$_2$O isotopologues.

Why isotopes?

  • In protoplanetary discs, ion-exchange reactions and isotope-selective photodissociation can fractionate $^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$ between gas and ice, so a planet’s atmospheric ratio may depend on birth location relative to snow lines.
  • In the Galaxy, $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{16}\mathrm{O}$ are primary products of helium burning, while $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ is largely a secondary CNO-cycle product. Trends of isotope ratios with metallicity therefore test Galactic chemical evolution (GCE) models — including the role of novae as $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ sources.

My contribution

Planets and brown dwarfs (ESO SupJup and follow-ups)

  • Measured $^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$ in young brown dwarfs with CRIRES+, including TWA 28 ($81^{+28}{-19}$) and J0856 ($79^{+20}{-14}$), consistent with the local ISM and favouring fragmentation-like formation (SupJup II).
  • Extended this programme to GQ Lup B and its host star (SupJup IV) and to the directly imaged planet β Pic b ($58^{+18}_{-15}$; A&A 2026).
  • With JWST/NIRSpec, constrained both carbon and oxygen isotopes in TWA 27A and TWA 28 from the CO fundamental and water isotopologues (A&A 2025).

Cool stars

  • Led a Nature Astronomy study of 32 nearby M dwarfs, detecting $^{13}\mathrm{CO}$ in nearly the full sample and finding that more metal-rich stars have lower $^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$ — matching GCE models with ongoing $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ enrichment from novae (NatAs 2025).

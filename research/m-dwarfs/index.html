M dwarfs

M dwarfs make up roughly three-quarters of stars in the Milky Way. They are long-lived, chemically relatively pristine after formation, and their cool atmospheres are rich in molecular lines — including the CO features needed to measure rare carbon and oxygen isotopes.

Scientific motivation

Elemental abundances alone leave degeneracies in Galactic chemical evolution. Isotope ratios add orthogonal constraints because different isotopes form through distinct channels: primary helium burning versus secondary CNO processing (and, for $^{18}\mathrm{O}$, metallicity- and rotation-dependent yields in massive stars). Until recently, precise $^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{16}\mathrm{O}/^{18}\mathrm{O}$ measurements in M dwarfs were scarce.

My contribution

In González Picos, Snellen & de Regt (2025, Nature Astronomy) we analysed archival SPIRou K-band spectra of 32 nearby M dwarfs (within ~15 pc), spanning [M/H] from about −0.4 to +0.4:

  • $^{13}\mathrm{CO}$ was detected at $>3\sigma$ in 29/32 stars; $\mathrm{C}^{18}\mathrm{O}$ in a subset, with upper limits elsewhere.
  • We find a clear trend of decreasing $^{12}\mathrm{C}/^{13}\mathrm{C}$ with increasing metallicity, consistent with GCE models in which novae enrich the ISM in $^{13}\mathrm{C}$ over the past few Gyr.
  • Super-solar metallicity stars approach present-day ISM-like carbon isotope ratios (~68), while $^{16}\mathrm{O}/^{18}\mathrm{O}$ in the most metal-rich objects can fall well below solar values.
  • Atmospheric models used petitRADTRANS with FastChem equilibrium chemistry and free abundance offsets, achieving ~1–2% relative fit precision across the CO band.

These results establish M dwarfs as practical tracers of chemical enrichment and motivate extension to lower metallicity with ELT/METIS and JWST.
